# Research and Technical Communication

Use this family when the audience mainly needs to understand a finding, mechanism, or technical domain and judge what can be concluded. Preserve the depth already supported by the existing guides; this family selects among them rather than replacing them with a generic outline.

## Load the relevant depth guide

- When learners must read a program, predict or explain its behavior, correct a misconception, or apply the idea in code, read [Programming instruction](programming-instruction.md), including for a short introductory classroom lesson. Select by this learning outcome, not the mere presence of code. Keep an expert implementation/optimization talk on the deep-dive route below; a full curriculum or a procedural setup manual needs its own inferred scope.
- When a Deck's primary job is to turn a complex technical domain into an audience-readable overview, relationship model, or domain-level judgment—such as a technical sharing, industry landscape, capability framework, standards brief, or trend analysis—read `systemic-technical-briefing.md`. Do not route a Deck merely because its subject is technical.
- When a Deck's primary job is to help a technical audience form a usable mental model of how one concrete system, algorithm, implementation, or optimization works—and connect that model to traceable code, formulas, execution behavior, or benchmarks—read `technical-deep-dive.md`. This is a depth-first expert-talk route, not a breadth-first domain overview; do not route a Deck merely because technical artifacts appear.
- When a Deck's primary job is to communicate empirical research or a formal policy, program, management, clinical, or model evaluation—and the audience must judge what the evidence supports, how credible the inference is, and where its uncertainty or applicability ends—read `research-evidence-briefing.md` before the affected Page Briefs are finalized. Apply only the relevant page-level guidance when such material is one section of a broader Deck. Do not route a Deck merely because it contains data, charts, citations, medical content, scientific terminology, an engineering benchmark, an ordinary KPI review, or a source labelled as research, and do not let this route override a user-locked source-faithful page order or independent-page boundary.

At Deck level, use the specialized reference that best matches the primary audience job. Apply another only to a bounded section or page with that genuine secondary job, without creating a second communication contract or visual grammar. A short explanation that needs none of these guides can stay with the shared workflow; brevity alone does not skip programming instruction when code reasoning is the learning outcome.

## Keep the question central

Derive the sequence from what the audience must understand or judge: why the question matters, the necessary model or method, the decisive observation or behavior, and the source-supported implications and limits. These are reasoning needs, not a mandatory slide roster. Keep comparisons, equations, code, and uncertainty only where they do real explanatory work; do not add them to make a deck appear academic.

For a mechanism, connect an intelligible model to actual inputs, transitions, outputs, code, or measurements. For a research finding, preserve the claim's population, comparison, units, uncertainty, and inference boundary. For a landscape, explain meaningful relationships and trade-offs rather than listing names.

For a classroom-like technical talk, a repeated header, ordinary bullets, readable code, incremental worked examples, and complete static states may be the clearest visual expression. Choose that expression through the classroom direction in [style profiles](style-profiles.md), independently of the content task. That choice does not require a formal research argument and does not claim a complete curriculum-design module.

## Avoid drifting into a status report

An implementation milestone can establish where an example came from, but a list of completed tickets does not explain a mechanism. Conversely, when the main audience question is whether a research or engineering project is on track, use [work progress report](work-progress-report.md) as the primary family and keep scientific detail bounded to what changes that judgment.

Choose the visual direction separately through [style profiles](style-profiles.md). Retain the shared source, diagram, composition, QA, workbench, and offline-delivery workflow.
