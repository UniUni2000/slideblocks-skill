# Style Profiles

Choose visual expression independently of the content task in `task-routing.md`. Academic / Scientific and Product Launch have been exercised by real Decks in the current Registry; Report / Briefing and Classroom / Handout below are scoped starting directions, not verified Registry Deck profiles. These are adaptable directions, not fixed templates, task labels, or a closed menu. Explicit user style, accepted templates, applicable tastes, actual material, and viewing context determine the fit.

## Separate quality from style

Keep these requirements non-negotiable across every direction:

- Preserve facts, evidence, citations, and asset provenance.
- Keep primary content readable at the real 1280 × 720 viewport.
- Prevent clipping, collisions, occlusion, and hidden required content.
- Keep formulas, charts, diagrams, and motion clear, legible, unambiguous, and professionally finished in their final rendered context.
- Provide complete reduced-motion and stable print/export states.
- Complete the checks in `qa.md` and obey `quality-contract.md`.

Allow the user or selected direction to override palette, background tone, type personality, composition, image treatment, ornament, and motion intensity when the quality floor remains intact.

## House starting direction across tasks

Start with high useful density in every task family. On a content page, give the audience room to read the comparisons, mechanisms, examples or qualifications that answer its question together. Use grouping, alignment and modest headings so detail remains readable. A cover, a live demonstration or a single-object reveal can carry its density in a diagram or demonstration rather than additional prose. Facts and viewing distance determine the stopping point.

Treat high information density as a body-page goal during selection, composition and visual review. Gather related baselines, differences, causes, conditions and consequences before compressing the story. Compare the details and relationships visible together with the selected professional page. Compact readable type, bullets, aligned rows and local figure explanations can carry more of the relevant evidence. Review density, reading order and typographic craft together before accepting the study; an enlarged sparse summary still needs work.

For Chinese, first try **LXGW WenKai Regular** for running text and headings. Use the bundled [font preparation and offline verification instructions](classroom-handout-visuals.md#type-hierarchy-and-a-real-cjk-font) independently of the classroom layout. Use tabular numerals for quantitative columns and a suitable monospace face for code. Compare the actual glyphs at delivery size. An explicit type choice, a supplied brand or a demonstrated legibility problem takes precedence. Use the selected language's own type system for other scripts.

## Product Launch

Verified by `decks/macbook-pro-m5-launch`.

- Build an image- or product-led narrative with large claims and restrained supporting copy.
- Use cinematic continuity only when the object, camera, or lighting needs to persist across pages.
- Carry adjacent scenes through focal anchors, physical seams, scale, translation, perspective, or masks; do not use a full-slide opacity fade as the main transition.
- Let each visible feature or motion prove the claim it accompanies.
- Keep persistent chrome sparse and hold a stable final composition.
- Snap reduced-motion, overview, print, and export to complete readable states.

Vary light or dark treatment, brand color, typography, image density, and motion amplitude to fit the product and audience.

## Academic / Scientific

Verified by `decks/sgr-a-discovery`.

- Carry the scientific-talk craft into non-research topics: use authentic product images, interfaces, photographs, documents or appropriate illustrative assets in place of paper figures. Do not invent research framing, equations or data merely to look academic.
- Collect and inspect useful visual material before fixing composition. Give imagery the main canvas weight through large source panels, meaningful crops, close-ups or coordinated visual groups. If a source figure is dense, crop a relevant panel, enlarge it or distribute the explanation across permitted pages before rejecting it for an arbitrary layout region.
- Use concise ordinary bullets to connect observations, mechanisms, comparisons and implications to the visible material. Keep node labels short and move explanation into the nearby bullets; preserve necessary qualifiers and source context. Scientific style is established by material, relationships and composition, not by a paper-colored background or serif font alone.
- When the research argument is central, make its claim, evidence, quantitative result, interpretation, and source relationship clear. Let showcase, orientation, transition, and single-object pages follow their own purpose instead of forcing that sequence onto every slide.
- Answer one complete question per body page with enough related evidence and explanation. A multi-panel paper figure, a comparison, or a figure/formula/schematic combination can form the visual center; do not force a single dominant image with a small text sidebar on every page.
- Use accurate figures, semantic KaTeX, readable axes and legends, local annotations, and adjacent credits.
- Reveal conclusions only when the evidence supports them; keep future evidence fully hidden beforehand.
- Use a white or paper-like canvas, restrained palette, scholarly title treatment, and sans-serif labels when they fit the brand, source material, audience, viewing context, and content-specific visual thesis; choose another coherent treatment when the content supports it more strongly.
- Preserve mathematical correctness, evidence integrity, legibility, and figure clearance under every stylistic override.
- Follow the canonical Academic and Scientific Slides and Scientific Figure and Visual QA guidance in `quality-contract.md` for useful density, mathematics, source-figure selection, short adjacent citations, and poster-like openings/focused endings. Do not use stylistic restraint as a reason to remove necessary evidence or create sparse body slides.

Vary palette, background, editorial character, illustration treatment, and interaction density when the research argument remains clear and source-backed.

## Report / Briefing

Reference-informed starting direction; not yet a verified Registry profile. See the original examples and page-specific lessons in `work-progress-report.md`. It may serve a progress report, a technical decision brief, or another audience job that benefits from quick comparison.

When this direction fits, read [Report / Briefing visual direction](report-briefing-visuals.md) before finalizing the affected Page Briefs. It extracts methods from real Tesla, Tencent, Wikimedia, and NASA content pages and helps select progress, comparison, output, and action carriers from the available evidence.

- Start from stable alignment, clear sans-serif headings, readable body text, and restrained emphasis. A light canvas with dark ink and a limited accent palette is useful when no brand or accepted direction suggests otherwise; adapt it freely to a supplied dark or branded template.
- Let the important result or observation lead. Keep the chart, actual deliverable, annotated screenshot, schedule, or comparison at a useful scale, with explanation close to the material it qualifies.
- Make parallel comparisons visually comparable: consistent units, time ranges, baselines, label positions, and semantic colors. State remains understandable without color.
- Vary the composition for summary, evidence, progress, explanation, and next actions. Do not require card grids, giant KPI numerals, or a dashboard on every page.
- Tune density for the afterlife: a spoken update needs projection-readable labels; a read-ahead can carry more detail. Do not reproduce tiny investor tables or gray-on-white body copy by imitation.
- Keep animation purposeful and modest when the audience needs fast assessment. Hold a complete static state with the evidence and its limitations visible.

This direction's look is a local hypothesis until reviewed in real output. Do not promote a sample's palette, font, or furniture into a shared quality requirement.

## Classroom / Handout

For teacher-led reading, worked code, concepts, conditions and examples that need stable reading anchors across pages, read [Classroom / Handout visual direction](classroom-handout-visuals.md). Recommend it for an unbranded programming lesson when no accepted deck, explicit style or applicable taste points elsewhere. It also fits a technical lecture with that reading job; a teaching subject alone does not require it.

This direction supplies a compact, readable type hierarchy, a local CJK font path and stable page structure. It supports text-only pages, source/terminal material, native local annotations and complete handout states. Adapt composition to the relationship being taught; preserve the existing image/figure treatment when real imagery is useful. Other directions can reuse the house WenKai treatment independently of the classroom background, type scale and page structure.

## Selecting and synthesizing a direction

Choose one strongest direction by adapting a fitting profile or synthesizing a custom direction with the actual story and the user's applied tastes from `taste-profile.md`. A reference-informed starting direction remains a custom direction in the project record. Present alternatives only when the user explicitly asks for variants or when its cold-start sample step applies.

These profiles are not a closed style list. When no verified profile fits, use `design-principles.md` to synthesize a custom direction from the content, audience, brand, selected Registry Artifacts, and communication goal. Record it as a custom direction; do not name it as a verified profile until a real Registry Deck has exercised and passed it.
