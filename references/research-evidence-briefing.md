# Research Evidence Briefing

Use this reference when the Deck's primary job is to communicate empirical research or a formal policy, program, management, clinical, or model evaluation, and the audience must judge what the evidence supports, how credible the inference is, and where its uncertainty or applicability ends. Apply only the relevant page-level guidance when a broader Deck contains one genuine research-results section.

Do not use it merely because the sources contain numbers, charts, medical content, scientific terminology, or an academic citation. A source-faithful migration, fixed exhibition sequence, product presentation, historical archive, or ordinary operational update keeps its accepted page responsibilities unless a page genuinely asks the audience to evaluate an empirical result. User-locked page count, order, source boundaries, and independent-page requirements remain controlling.

This is conditional reasoning guidance inside the existing communication contract, source boundary, flexible Page Briefs, Visual Evidence Plan, composition plan, and QA. It does not change First-Run Project Routing, select a style profile, create a second visual grammar or research plan, add a schema field or score, set a fixed page ratio, impose a title formula, or define a reusable page layout. When another specialized briefing reference also appears relevant, choose the Deck-level reference from the primary audience job and apply the other only to a bounded section or page with that genuine secondary job.

PowerPoint migration and the recorded source-fidelity boundary remain upstream. This guidance may clarify an existing conclusion, choose a clearer evidence carrier, or expose source-supported uncertainty within the mapped destination page. It does not authorize a new analysis, a stronger causal claim, cross-page content movement, or a merge, split, reorder, or conclusion absent from the accepted sources. In an exhibition, institutional-history, showcase, or documentary Deck, apply only the relevant descriptive-result guidance to a page whose actual job is analytical interpretation; do not manufacture a research narrative, model, confounder analysis, or claim chain.

## Derive the statement before leading with it

Keep analysis order and presentation order distinct:

- **Analysis order:** audience question → study design and evidence → statement the evidence can support.
- **Presentation order:** supported statement → decisive evidence → credibility and applicability boundary.

Never start from a preferred conclusion and search for supporting material. Classify the inference actually supported by the sources—description, association, prediction, or causal/effect estimation—and do not strengthen one into another. Preserve the original study population, period, comparison, outcome definitions, uncertainty, and stated limitations.

Preserve null, mixed, contrary, subgroup-specific, and time-varying findings when they materially change interpretation. Do not remove them before carrier selection merely to simplify the message.

A result page may lead with a conclusion-style title only when the evidence and design support that wording. When the inference remains unresolved, use a precise question, observation, or bounded statement rather than manufacturing certainty for rhetorical force.

## Allocate attention by audience judgment

Allocate page count, presenter time, and visual weight according to the importance of the audience's judgment and the evidence burden needed to support it, not according to the length or order of the source document. Protect enough attention for the principal result, how it was obtained, the most material credibility question, and the limit on interpretation.

Compress background and procedural detail that does not change interpretation. Keep method, baseline, denominator, or validation detail visible when it is necessary to understand or trust the result. Do not set a universal proportion of background, method, and result pages. When the user locks page count or order, improve emphasis within those boundaries rather than adding, moving, merging, or splitting pages.

Apply Deck-level attention allocation and contact-sheet peak checks only when this is the Deck's primary specialized reference and the Deck has an ordered research argument. In that case, the principal result or decision peak should be identifiable from hierarchy and evidence weight. For a bounded section, one routed page, or an independent-page Deck, judge each affected page in its own viewing context without comparing it with unrelated page responsibilities. This does not require every page to become a result page or every Deck to build a page-to-page argument.

## Plan result pages around a judgment

For a page whose job is to establish or qualify a research judgment, selectively record the following in its existing flexible Page Brief:

- the audience question the page must answer;
- the clearest statement the current sources can support;
- the observation, comparison, or estimate that supports that statement;
- the design, adjustment, validation, uncertainty, or alternative explanation that most affects credibility;
- the boundary on interpretation or applicability;
- the detail that belongs on screen, with the presenter, or in an appendix when those layers exist.

These are planning prompts, not mandatory fields, visible labels, or a five-part layout. Do not apply them to covers, transitions, archival showcases, quotations, orientation pages, or other pages whose purpose is not to establish a result. One page keeps one dominant communication responsibility, but it may use several dimensions of evidence when they support the same judgment.

Do not duplicate the Visual Evidence Plan. The Page Brief owns the reasoning and communication responsibility; the source map owns facts, provenance, and truth boundaries; the Visual Evidence Plan owns the evidence need, carrier-only acceptance test, option evaluation, selected source-map references, and evidence role.

## Follow the reader's reasoning, not the analysis output order

Use only the reasoning links the actual research question and sources require. The following are useful paths, not fixed page sequences:

- **Descriptive or trend analysis:** statistical scope and denominator → overall state → meaningful strata or composition → trend or change → exception, comparability issue, or bounded takeaway.
- **Association analysis:** population and variable definitions → observed relationship and uncertainty → adjustment or alternative explanations → remaining association and interpretation boundary.
- **Policy, intervention, or causal evaluation:** comparison and identification basis → baseline comparability where material → primary effect, magnitude, direction, and uncertainty → heterogeneity → confounding or sensitivity checks → residual limitation and applicability.
- **Predictive modelling:** target and population → validation design → discrimination, calibration, or error in the relevant units → subgroup or external performance where available → generalization boundary.

Combine related findings when doing so lowers the audience's comparison cost. Preserve separate pages or regions when outcomes, units, estimands, populations, or credibility questions are not honestly comparable. Use a summary → supporting findings → bounded synthesis structure only when it matches how the audience must form the judgment; never impose it on an independent-page Deck.

Use count and proportion according to the question. Lead with count when scale is the judgment, then add composition only if it changes interpretation. Lead with proportion when composition is the judgment, but preserve the denominator. Do not show both by habit, and do not treat them as interchangeable measures.

## Make the principal model interpretable

Apply this section only when the sources contain a genuine principal model or primary effect analysis. Prefer one coherent primary-model page for one principal research question when it remains legible, but do not force all models onto one slide or split a user-locked page merely to satisfy this preference.

Make the following recoverable from the page and its intrinsic evidence labels as applicable:

- the research question or target estimand;
- the analysis population, period, and sample size when supplied;
- the primary outcome, intervention, exposure, or predictor;
- the comparison, reference group, or baseline;
- the main estimate's direction, magnitude, unit, and source-provided uncertainty;
- whether the estimate is unadjusted, adjusted, validated, stratified, or otherwise conditioned;
- the meaningful groups of adjustment variables and the source-supported status or grouping of multiple outcomes, when one exists;
- the most informative robustness comparison and the remaining limitation.

Show a formula only when it helps the audience understand the model or estimate. Do not shrink a complete regression output into the slide. Move exhaustive coefficients or specifications to presenter material or an appendix when the delivery supports those layers. Never invent a missing confidence interval, covariate, comparison group, sensitivity analysis, or model status.

Compare unadjusted, adjusted, and sensitivity estimates on one scale only when they share the same estimand, unit, and interpretable reference. Separate outcomes or tracks when units or meanings differ.

## Expose the credibility chain

Choose credibility content from the inference type rather than adding a generic robustness module:

- For descriptive results, inspect denominator, statistical period, definitions, missingness, data coverage, and comparability across groups or years.
- For association results, inspect measurement, adjustment, plausible alternative explanations, uncertainty, and whether the wording remains associational.
- For policy, intervention, or causal claims, inspect the comparison and identification assumptions, baseline imbalance, confounding, selection, concurrent changes, uncertainty, heterogeneity, and sensitivity analyses actually performed.
- For predictive results, inspect train/validation separation, validation population, discrimination, calibration, error, subgroup performance, and external generalization when supplied.

When robustness or confounding materially affects the conclusion, make the chain visible:

> threat to interpretation → handling or test → change relative to the principal result → residual limitation

Including covariates does not prove that all confounding was removed. Repeating statistical significance does not by itself establish robustness, a similar direction across models does not prove causality, and an interval crossing the null does not by itself prove no effect. Distinguish prespecified and exploratory subgroup analysis when the source does. Show only checks that were actually performed; unresolved threats remain explicit boundaries rather than being rewritten as stability.

## Let charts and tables answer the question

Choose the evidence form from the comparison or judgment, not from the chart already present in the source.

- Preserve the time period, unit, denominator, baseline or reference, sample, uncertainty, category definition, and source needed to interpret the result.
- Make the direction of improvement, harm, increase, decrease, or ambiguity explicit when the measure has such a direction.
- Do not place unlike units, outcomes, estimands, or incompatible populations on one quantitative axis.
- Disclose a truncated baseline or other transformation that could visually amplify a difference.
- Show absolute and relative effects together only when both are source-supported and the distinction materially affects interpretation.
- Use a table when exact lookup or multivariable correspondence matters. Do not use a screenshot of complete statistical output as the main result carrier.
- Remove rows and columns unrelated to the page judgment while preserving reference groups, sample sizes, units, adjustment state, uncertainty, and necessary footnotes.
- Use consistent decimal precision and avoid visual precision that exceeds the source or measurement.
- Do not duplicate the same evidence in a chart and table unless one genuinely reveals the pattern and the other is needed for exact values.

Highlight only the marks or cells that carry the current comparison. Emphasis selects the reading path; it must not hide contrary, null, or qualifying evidence that changes the conclusion.

## Final rendered checks

For this route, the following failures block approval:

- the primary result cannot be found quickly in its full-size page, routed section, or complete contact sheet as applicable;
- a title or takeaway is stronger than the data, design, or uncertainty supports;
- in a Deck-level application or ordered research section, background or procedural method receives the dominant attention while the principal result, how it was obtained, or its credibility is compressed;
- a result or evaluation page presents data without the research judgment it exists to establish or qualify, or presents that judgment without checkable supporting evidence;
- description, association, prediction, effect, and causality are used interchangeably;
- a quantitative carrier omits a necessary unit, denominator, reference, period, uncertainty, sample context, or source;
- a robustness claim is not tied to a concrete threat, handling or test, observed change, and remaining limit;
- multiple outcomes obscure their source-supported status or grouping, or incompatible results share a scale or visual encoding;
- the essential result or credibility boundary exists only in presenter narration, hover, animation, or an unavailable interaction state.

During rendered review, ask whether an audience member can state the supported finding, identify what was compared, see why the evidence is credible enough for that wording, and name the principal boundary. When this is the primary route for an ordered research argument, inspect whether the contact sheet gives the principal result attention proportional to its decision importance; for a bounded or independent-page application, inspect the affected page in its own viewing context without forcing unrelated pages into one argument pattern. Treat these as qualitative judgments, not a score, ratio, mandatory page count, or title template. Repair the highest-impact failed page and recheck the affected sequence or independent-page set.
